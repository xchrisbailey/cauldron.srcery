# Cauldron native: imports (Distill)

The pipeline is pure and lives in `apple/CauldronKit/Sources/CauldronKit/Imports/` (`Distill.url`, `Distill.text`; its two seams are `PageFetcher` and `RecipeExtracting` in `Seams.swift`). The flow around it is the `CauldronImports` framework, `apple/CauldronImports/Imports/` (it has no views, and the share extension links it too): `URLSessionPageFetcher`, `ImportModel` (the flow), `CoverPhoto`, and `RecipeDraft+Import` (the pipeline's draft becomes the editor's draft; nothing is stored until the cook saves). The views and what needs the store are `apple/Cauldron/Imports/`: `ImportSheet` and `DuplicateLinks`. A debug-only list of live links to try is in `ImportLiveChecks.swift`.

## The fetcher's rules

`AddressRules` is a device-sized version of `RemoteFetch.ts`'s address checks, applied to the first request, every redirect hop and the cover image, all of which go through `URLSessionPageFetcher.checked`. A page can point a redirect or its cover image at the cook's router or printer, and a GET there can have side effects. So it refuses: a port other than the scheme's default; `localhost` and names ending `.local`, `.localhost`, `.internal`, `.lan`, `.home.arpa`; IP-literal hosts in loopback, private, link-local, CGNAT, unspecified, multicast and reserved ranges (IPv4 in any spelling `URL` keeps, such as `2130706433`, `0x7f.1` or `127.1`, and IPv6 including IPv4-mapped forms). Each is `FetchError.blocked`, so the cook gets "can't open that link" and the paste fallback. It judges the link as written: a public name that resolves to a private address is not caught, because the device has no resolver to ask.

Plain-http links rely on App Transport Security refusing them (`blocked("insecure")`). Nothing may add an ATS exception to the app without restoring a check in `AddressRules`, because ATS is the only thing stopping a cleartext request to the local network today.

## The macros of an import

As the web import did (`withMacros` in `distill.ts`), a recipe whose source published no nutrition has its macros estimated during the import. `Distilled.macroEstimate` is the ask (built by `MacroEstimateInput.limited`, so held to the web's limits); `ImportModel` makes it beside the cover photo, within `DistillOptions.macroTimeout` (30 s), through `RecipeMacroEstimates.divine`. The numbers go into the draft's macro fields, marked as an estimate (`estimatedMacros`, so Save records them as `.estimated`) and flagged for the cook to check and edit. Nutrition the page published always wins, and nothing is asked then.

When the estimate can't be had (no key, a refused key, no credit, offline, too slow, or an answer with nothing usable) the fields stay empty and the draft is marked `estimateOnSave`: Save queues the estimate for the saved recipe, which runs when the provider can be reached (decision 10: queued, never guessed). Numbers the cook types, or clears, before saving stand in its place.

## The cover photo

`CoverPhoto` fetches it through the same fetcher: at most 15 MB, `Accept: image/*`, and 20 seconds for the fetch and the decode together (a decode still running at the limit is left to finish and its result dropped). An image from a page may declare at most 24 megapixels, checked from its header before decoding, because a file under 100 KB can declare 100 megapixels; the picker path keeps its own 100 megapixel cap. A photo that fails for any reason is dropped silently and never fails the import.

## From a photo

A third way into the same sheet (#172): photograph a cookbook page or recipe card. The pages are read on the device and the text goes down the paste path, so everything after it (the draft in the editor, unsure fields flagged, a rough reading warned about, the macro estimate) is the same as for pasted text, with the source recorded as pasted text and no link.

- **Getting pages in.** iPhone and iPad: VisionKit's document scanner (several pages in one go, each straightened) or the photo library. Mac: the photo library, image files from the open panel, or files dropped on the sheet. There is no scanner on the Mac. At most ten pages; a picture over the photo picker's 100 megapixel cap (checked from its header) or 15 MB is refused with the same words the picker uses. The scanner closing without saving is a cancel. A device with no scanner, or a refused camera, says so in plain words and leaves the library and pasting open. Pages stay in memory: nothing is written to disk and no image leaves the device.
- **Reading.** `VisionTextReader` (behind `RecipeTextReader`) uses the Swift Vision API, `RecognizeTextRequest`, at the accurate level with language correction and automatic language detection, on a picture shrunk to 4096 px on its long side so a 48 megapixel photo doesn't fill memory. It runs off the main actor, one page at a time (the wait says "Page 1 of 2"), and honours Stop.
- **Reading order.** Vision returns lines with boxes in no useful order. `ReadingOrder` (CauldronKit, pure) puts them right: a clear vertical gutter makes columns, read left in full then right, with headings and footers that span them in their place; otherwise top to bottom with pieces on one baseline joined. Its rules are written at the top of `ReadingOrder.swift`. Pages are joined in the order captured with a blank line between, and handed to `Distill.text` as a paste is.
- **The first page as the photo.** A switch beside the pages, off by default, makes the first page's picture the recipe's photo (downscaled as the editor does it). The draft's photo row can still remove it.
- **Failures.** No text found, text over `Distill.textMax`, a page that can't be read, and no recipe in the text each get their own plain words and offer "Try again" (the pages as they were) and "Paste the text instead" (what was read comes along to fix, when it fits).

### Not verified yet

- A real cookbook page photographed on an iPhone, the document scanner on a device, and how Vision orders a real two-column page. The reader is tested against a page drawn in the test, not photographed.

## From the share sheet

A fourth way in (#173), made a way to save in one step by #198: share a link or text to Cauldron from Safari, Instagram, TikTok or any app, see what was found, and keep it without opening the app.

The pieces are split by what they may touch. The import pipeline, the AI and the key store are the `CauldronImports` framework (`apple/CauldronImports`), which the app embeds and the extension links; it has no views and never opens SwiftData. The sheet's own code is `apple/ShareSheet`, compiled into the app and the extension only (the app's tests reach it through `@testable import Cauldron`). `apple/Shared` is compiled separately into every target that needs it (`Brand`, `AppGroupName`), so its types are different types in each module. The extension never opens the store; everything it leaves for the app is a file in the App Group.

- **The sheet** (`ShareSheetModel`, `ShareSheetView`, hosted by `apple/CauldronShare/ShareViewController`). `SharedItemReader` chooses the item (first web link, first text; a provider may hand over a `URL`, a string, an attributed string or UTF-8 bytes) and `SharedImportRoute` routes it as the app does. `ShareSheetModel` wraps `ImportModel`, the same logic as the Distill sheet, so a shared page gets the same fetcher, the same checks and the same estimate. Its phases are `reading`, `ready`, `saved`, `sent` and `failed`. The ready summary (variant B, #195) shows the title and site, the cover as a small thumbnail (`CoverMode.thumbnail`; the full-size photo is the app's job), ingredient and step counts, total time and servings in Geist Mono, the macros per serving, a banner when the recipe is already in the box, and the ingredient lines the import wasn't sure of. Save leaves the finished recipe; "Edit in Cauldron" (or "Fix it in Cauldron" when lines need checking) leaves the link or text for a draft. The Mac shows it in a fixed 380 by 520 popover, the ready state scrolling with Save pinned under it.
- **Every way out is safe.** The sheet closes only after what it is leaving is on disk, and anything that goes wrong offers "Send to Cauldron instead", which leaves the link or text for the app to open as a draft, as the extension did before the sheet. That covers a page that can't be read, a link `ImportModel` turns down (too long, or carrying credentials) and a run that ends cancelled without the cook cancelling; none of them is left on a spinner. A recipe that can't be written offers the same.
- **No AI key.** The sheet asks the cook's chosen provider only (`RecipeAIService.live(suiteName:)`, which reads the provider and model the app copies into the App Group's defaults whenever it comes forward or goes away). With no key for that provider the sheet doesn't fall back to the on-device model: a page the structured data or the heuristics can read still comes back as ready, and one that needs a model says so in plain words ("Cauldron needs an AI key to read this") with Send to Cauldron, where the app, which has the key, reads it. The offline fallback is the other case and is unchanged: a key is present but the provider can't be reached, so the on-device model reads it and the summary is marked rough. The sheet doesn't ask for the macro estimate in either of those cases; the macros are marked pending and the app asks once the recipe is saved.
- **Finished recipes** (`FinishedInbox`, `FinishedRecipe`). Save writes one JSON file per recipe, named for its id, into `FinishedRecipes/` in the App Group container (`AppGroupFolder`). The payload is the structured draft (`KeptDraft`: ingredients as read, not a blob of text), where it came from (platform, author, site name, fetch time, rough), the macros the page published, the `estimateOnSave` marker and `coverLink`, the link to the cover photo. It carries no photo: the extension has little memory to spare. It is a folder of its own because `ShareInbox.takeAll` deletes any file it can't read as a `SharedItem`. The file is written whole or not at all, and a payload of another version is dropped.
- **Receiving** (`FinishedRecipeReceiver`, called from `SharedImportQueue.pickUp`). Finished recipes go in before the links. Each goes through `RecipeSaver.save` with the payload's own id as the recipe's id, so a payload received twice (a crash between the save and the delete) finds its recipe and inserts nothing. The payload's file is removed only after the save, or, when it has a cover link, after the cover is fetched the way an import fetches it (`CoverPhoto`), so the recipe is in the box at once and the photo follows. A photo that can't be had leaves the recipe without one. A recipe banished since stays banished. The macro estimate the sheet couldn't ask for is queued as an import's is, and runs at once if the AI can be reached. A payload the saver won't take (the sheet's check and the saver's disagree) falls back to a link left for a draft rather than being lost.
- **Saved links** (`SavedLinksIndex`). The sheet can't open the store, so it can't ask whether a recipe is already in the box. The app writes `SavedLinks.json` in the App Group: the keys of every recipe's source link that isn't banished, rewritten wherever a recipe is saved, edited, banished or restored, and at launch and each time the app comes forward. The sheet reads it, using the same rules as the app's own check (`SavedLinks`, `DuplicateLinks`), so the two can't drift; the index holds links, not recipes, so the banner can say "You already have this one" but not open it, and Save then reads "Save another copy". An index that is missing or unreadable means no banner.
- **Picking up.** `SharedImportQueue` takes both folders at launch and each time the app comes forward (`sharedImports(_:)` in `CauldronApp`), and does nothing under test (`CauldronStore.launchMode == .inMemory`). Links and text are removed from the inbox as they are read. On the Mac, scene changes aren't enough: the popover can save a recipe while the window is open and in front. `FolderWatcher` watches `FinishedRecipes/` for as long as the app is open and takes finished recipes the moment they land. It watches the folder instead of posting a Darwin notification because both sides already share the folder and there is no name to keep in step or entitlement to ask for. Only finished recipes are taken this way: a link left for a draft waits for the app to come forward, so no sheet opens by itself.
- **The AI key.** The extension needs the cook's key, so the key lives in a keychain access group both the app and the extension are in (`AIKeyGroup.shared`, from the Info.plist, where signing fills in the team prefix; `KeychainKeyStore` reads and writes it by default). A key saved before the group existed is in the app's own group (`AIKeyGroup.legacy`). `AIKeyMove` moves it across, for each provider, at every launch and each time the app comes forward, before anything reads a key. That is safe to repeat: once moved there is nothing left to move; a move the keychain refuses (locked before the first unlock) is tried again; a key another device saved with an older build moves when iCloud Keychain brings it; on the Mac a key kept in the file-based keychain is added to the group before it is removed; and when both groups hold a key, the one in the old group is newer (an older build only writes there) and replaces the other. The key is never read or moved under test, and a build with no team has no group and keeps the key where the system puts it. The Mac extension also has `com.apple.security.network.client`, so it can fetch pages in its sandbox.
- **Routing** (`SharedImportRoute`). A web link takes the link path, so the duplicate check, the paste fallback and the cover photo all apply; any text that came with it (Safari sends the page title) is ignored. An Instagram or TikTok link (`Distill.classify`) with a caption takes the pasted-text path with the post kept as the source (`ImportModel.pastingFor`, so the draft records the platform and link); the link is taken out of the caption first, and a post with no caption left takes the link path. Text alone takes the paste path, unless the text is only a link, which takes the link path.
- **One at a time.** A link or text left for a draft is opened by `RecipesRoot` when `readyToOpen`: something is waiting, no import sheet is open and no `RecipeEditorSheet` is open (editors register themselves with the queue). `AppShell` switches to Recipes at that moment. A short pause lets a sheet that has just closed finish going. While one is open the sheet shows "2 more shared". Nothing is saved until the cook saves.
- **The extension's look.** The sheet draws with `Brand.Colors`: `Shared/Brand.xcassets` is named as a resource of the share target in `project.yml`, as the widget's is (so its Info.plist lives in `Config/`). Geist is not copied into the extension; `ShareFonts` reads it from the app that contains the extension (found by walking up from the extension's bundle to the `.app`) and falls back to the system font if it isn't there. The sheet's buttons are `ShareButtonStyle`, which `make lint-buttons` keeps free of `.borderedProminent`, as it does the app's.

## The Mac's file entitlement

The Mac app is sandboxed. Choosing image files and dropping them onto the import sheet need `com.apple.security.files.user-selected.read-only`, which is in `apple/Cauldron/Cauldron-macOS.entitlements`. It lets the app read only the files the cook picks or drops, and nothing else. The app never needs write access to files the cook chooses.
