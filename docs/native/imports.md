# Cauldron native: imports (Distill)

The pipeline is pure and lives in `apple/CauldronKit/Sources/CauldronKit/Imports/` (`Distill.url`, `Distill.text`; its two seams are `PageFetcher` and `RecipeExtracting` in `Seams.swift`). The flow around it is the `CauldronImports` framework, `apple/CauldronImports/Imports/` (it has no views, and the share extension links it too): `URLSessionPageFetcher`, `ImportModel` (the flow), `CoverPhoto`, and `RecipeDraft+Import` (the pipeline's draft becomes the editor's draft; nothing is stored until the cook saves). The views and what needs the store are `apple/Cauldron/Imports/`: `ImportSheet` and `DuplicateLinks`. A debug-only list of live links to try is in `ImportLiveChecks.swift`.

## The fetcher's rules

`AddressRules` is a device-sized version of `RemoteFetch.ts`'s address checks, applied to the first request, every redirect hop and the cover image, all of which go through `URLSessionPageFetcher.checked`. A page can point a redirect or its cover image at the cook's router or printer, and a GET there can have side effects. So it refuses: a port other than the scheme's default; `localhost` and names ending `.local`, `.localhost`, `.internal`, `.lan`, `.home.arpa`; IP-literal hosts in loopback, private, link-local, CGNAT, unspecified, multicast and reserved ranges (IPv4 in any spelling `URL` keeps, such as `2130706433`, `0x7f.1` or `127.1`, and IPv6 including IPv4-mapped forms). Each is `FetchError.blocked`, so the cook gets "can't open that link" and the paste fallback. It judges the link as written: a public name that resolves to a private address is not caught, because the device has no resolver to ask.

Plain-http links rely on App Transport Security refusing them (`blocked("insecure")`). Nothing may add an ATS exception to the app without restoring a check in `AddressRules`, because ATS is the only thing stopping a cleartext request to the local network today.

## The macros of an import

As the web import did (`withMacros` in `distill.ts`), a recipe whose source published no nutrition has its macros estimated during the import. `Distilled.macroEstimate` is the ask (built by `MacroEstimateInput.limited`, so held to the web's limits); `ImportModel` makes it beside the cover photo, within `DistillOptions.macroTimeout` (30 s), through `RecipeMacroEstimates.divine`. The numbers go into the draft's macro fields, marked as an estimate (`estimatedMacros`, so Save records them as `.estimated`) and flagged for the cook to check and edit. Nutrition the page published always wins, and nothing is asked then.

When the estimate can't be had (no key, a refused key, no credit, offline, too slow, or an answer with nothing usable) the fields stay empty and the draft is marked `estimateOnSave`: Save queues the estimate for the saved recipe, which runs when the provider can be reached (decision 10: queued, never guessed). Numbers the cook types, or clears, before saving stand in its place.

## The cover photo

`CoverPhoto` fetches it through the same fetcher: at most 15 MB, `Accept: image/*`, and 20 seconds for the fetch and the decode together (a decode still running at the limit is left to finish and its result dropped). An image from a page may declare at most 24 megapixels, checked from its header before decoding, because a file under 100 KB can declare 100 megapixels; the picker path keeps its own 100 megapixel cap. A photo that fails for any reason is dropped silently and never fails the import.

## From a photo

A third way into the same sheet (#172): photograph a cookbook page or recipe card. The pages are read on the device and the text goes down the paste path, so everything after it (the draft in the editor, unsure fields flagged, a rough reading warned about, the macro estimate) is the same as for pasted text, with the source recorded as pasted text and no link.

- **Getting pages in.** iPhone and iPad: VisionKit's document scanner (several pages in one go, each straightened) or the photo library. Mac: the photo library, image files from the open panel, or files dropped on the sheet. There is no scanner on the Mac. At most ten pages; a picture over the photo picker's 100 megapixel cap (checked from its header) or 15 MB is refused with the same words the picker uses. The scanner closing without saving is a cancel. A device with no scanner, or a refused camera, says so in plain words and leaves the library and pasting open. Pages stay in memory: nothing is written to disk and no image leaves the device.
- **Reading.** `VisionTextReader` (behind `RecipeTextReader`) uses the Swift Vision API, `RecognizeTextRequest`, at the accurate level with language correction and automatic language detection, on a picture shrunk to 4096 px on its long side so a 48 megapixel photo doesn't fill memory. It runs off the main actor, one page at a time (the wait says "Page 1 of 2"), and honours Stop.
- **Reading order.** Vision returns lines with boxes in no useful order. `ReadingOrder` (CauldronKit, pure) puts them right: a clear vertical gutter makes columns, read left in full then right, with headings and footers that span them in their place; otherwise top to bottom with pieces on one baseline joined. Its rules are written at the top of `ReadingOrder.swift`. Pages are joined in the order captured with a blank line between, and handed to `Distill.text` as a paste is.
- **The first page as the photo.** A switch beside the pages, off by default, makes the first page's picture the recipe's photo (downscaled as the editor does it). The draft's photo row can still remove it.
- **Failures.** No text found, text over `Distill.textMax`, a page that can't be read, and no recipe in the text each get their own plain words and offer "Try again" (the pages as they were) and "Paste the text instead" (what was read comes along to fix, when it fits).

### Not verified yet

- A real cookbook page photographed on an iPhone, the document scanner on a device, and how Vision orders a real two-column page. The reader is tested against a page drawn in the test, not photographed.

## From the share sheet

Share a recipe link or text to Cauldron, correct its title, servings and prep/cook minutes in the summary, and Save or continue in the full editor. The [implementation specification](https://github.com/xchrisbailey/cauldron.srcery/issues/216) records the chosen Visible fields interaction and the platform-specific handoff.

The extension uses the same `CauldronImports` pipeline as the app and never opens SwiftData. `ShareSheetModel` owns the current editable `RecipeDraft`; both actions consume that value, not the original import snapshot. All four fields are immediately editable, including imported values. The summary keeps its source, cover thumbnail, ingredient/step disclosures, rough-reading and duplicate warnings, and macro context. Ingredients and steps are edited in the full app.

- **Validation and layout.** The full `RecipeDraft` validation applies: title is required; servings and minute fields are optional whole numbers in their existing ranges. Blank is unspecified, not zero. Missing title is flagged immediately; other errors show after leaving a field or attempting Save. Save stays visible and focuses the first editable problem; read-only problems explain the need to continue. The 380×520 Mac popover scrolls with actions pinned; accessibility sizes stack time fields. Numeric inputs and native focus/keyboard behavior remain platform controls.
- **Ordinary Save.** `FinishedRecipe` and `FinishedInbox` retain the corrected structured recipe for `FinishedRecipeReceiver`, without foregrounding Cauldron. Stable recipe identity protects against repeated receipt. Cover photos are fetched in the app; unavailable macro estimates remain queued, never guessed. `SavedLinksIndex` preserves the duplicate banner and explicit Save another copy behavior. Finished-recipe pickup and the Mac folder watcher remain separate from draft continuation.
- **Shared draft versus saved recipe.** Continuation keeps unfinished work, not a recipe in the box. `RetainedSharedDraft` and its atomic store retain original source, a stable handoff identity, a parsed draft when available, provenance, roughness, review flags, photo choices, macro state and cover reference. The store is separate from the old destructive raw inbox, finished recipes, and the unrelated single new-recipe draft. Reads and editor presentation never consume its files.
- **Write before completion.** The sheet persists before reporting a kept draft, trying an opening, or completing the extension request. A persistence failure leaves its current edits/source available with retry/cancel guidance. The extension does not rely on delayed work after request completion. Repeated clicks reuse one handoff identity rather than making another draft.
- **iPhone continuation.** Continue in Cauldron keeps the draft and says Open Cauldron to finish editing. Apple's documented Share extension APIs do not support directly launching the containing app; the implementation uses manual continuation and no launch workaround.
- **Mac continuation.** Edit in Cauldron retains the draft, then awaits a narrow `SharedDraftOpening` boundary. The live adapter uses public `NSWorkspace` URL opening directed at the containing installation, activating/reusing that installation and requesting the exact identity route. It prefers an existing main window and keeps the app's editor/import gate. A failed opening leaves the recovery guidance visible until Done; workspace success is dispatch evidence, not acknowledgement that a particular editor consumed the payload. Ordinary Save never requests opening.
- **Routing and existing work.** `AppLink.sharedDraft` carries identity only. `SharedImportQueue` resolves local retained data, accepts either activation/URL order, and prioritizes the explicitly requested identity without reordering unrelated waiting drafts. No incoming request dismisses existing import/editor work. Waiting notices remain visible in editors, including standalone Mac recipe windows, which share the same queue. Recipes navigation/presentation happens when safe.
- **Editor recovery.** `SharedDraftSession` checkpoints the entire edited recipe as it changes and when the editor/background lifecycle requires it. Closing keeps the shared draft, suppresses immediate reopening, and permits recovery on a later activation or explicit request. Discard is explicit. Save uses the handoff identity as the saved recipe identity and reconciles an already-saved record after a crash; durable terminal status suppresses delayed Save/Discard requests. Source-URL duplicate detection remains a separate user choice.
- **Failed reads and promotion.** An unreadable/no-key/offline shared source is retained under the same identity as original URL/text and retried in the app's existing import form. A successful read is persisted before the full editor becomes ready. If persistence fails after parsing, Retry keeps the parsed result without another extraction. Unsupported/damaged payloads preserve recoverable source and original evidence rather than being silently deleted; wholly unreadable input gets an understandable failure and explicit discard path.
- **Covers after continuation.** A saved retained draft with an imported cover reference is settled through the queue's cover fetch path without reimporting its page. Cancellation or a failed store write leaves pending work recoverable. Explicit photo Remove/Replace takes precedence over a late cover result, including after restart. The defaulted `RecipeRecord.coverDeclined` flag saves a removal in the same transaction as the recipe, so a crash or unavailable retained-draft folder cannot undo it; this is an additive schema change from the original no-schema-change plan. Imported ingredient and step review flags and manual timer choices survive continuation.
- **AI access and appearance.** The extension still reads the chosen provider/model through shared defaults and the shared Keychain group, with the existing legacy-key move. It does not ask an on-device model to replace a missing provider key; a present but unreachable provider can use the existing rough reading fallback. `ShareFonts` loads Geist from the containing app and the extension uses the shared Brand assets. These provider, macro and signing policies are unchanged.

### Verification limits

Automated tests use scripted services, isolated temporary folders, and CauldronStore-created in-memory contexts. They exercise model-to-serialized-handoff-to-receiver behavior, opening result doubles, restart/idempotency/failure paths and native rendering. A mock opener and SDK declarations do not establish signed host behavior.

Still requiring real-device/host verification: signed Mac Share popovers from real apps (cold/warm/frontmost/no-window/multiple-installation cases and completion/focus timing), iPhone manual continuation, actual keyboard avoidance and hardware-keyboard controls, VoiceOver/error announcements and Dynamic Type. Keep these checks distinct from automated suite results and native rendered screenshots. The existing key migration/shared Keychain and cross-device iCloud checks likewise require signed devices.

## The Mac's file entitlement

The Mac app is sandboxed. Choosing image files and dropping them onto the import sheet need `com.apple.security.files.user-selected.read-only`, which is in `apple/Cauldron/Cauldron-macOS.entitlements`. It lets the app read only the files the cook picks or drops, and nothing else. The app never needs write access to files the cook chooses.
